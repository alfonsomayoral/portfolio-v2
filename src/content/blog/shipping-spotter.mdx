> TL;DR — Four products in one app is harder than four separate
> apps. Pretrained foundation models save you a year of engineering
> if you use them right. App Store rejections are a process, not an
> emergency. Ship v0.1 to production, not v1.0.

## What Spotter AI is

[Spotter AI](/projects/spotter) is a fitness companion on iOS and
Android. Development started in June 2025. We launched in February
2026. The app bundles four products that most competitors sell
separately:

1. **Nutrition** — photo a plate, get macros back from GPT-4o
2. **Workout** — live gym tracking with sets, reps, weight, RPE, 1RM
3. **Social** — feed of workouts and meals from people you follow
4. **Streaks and gamification** — 60+ achievements, weekly leagues, XP

Built with Expo + React Native + Supabase + GPT-4o + RevenueCat. One
codebase, two stores, two languages. I led engineering and wrote most
of the code; two collaborators contributed to parts of the build.

## What surprised me

**The model is the easy part.** I came in expecting to optimize
prompts, retrieval, and food classification. GPT-4o handles 95% of
the food-analysis problem out of the box. The hard work is the
**other** 95% of the product: the rest timer that has to keep running
when the user&apos;s phone is locked, the streak that has to roll over at
midnight in the user&apos;s local timezone, the feed that has to scroll
at 60fps with images that haven&apos;t loaded yet.

**Foundation models save you a year.** Spotter does not train any
custom vision model. GPT-4o is pretrained on a food-recognition
corpus that no team of three could reproduce in a year, and the cost
per call is a fraction of the engineering cost of getting to
comparable accuracy. Where we needed to optimize cost, we added
cheaper paths (barcode lookup, manual entry with AI normalization)
rather than swapping the model.

**Four products in one app costs more than four separate apps.** Each
pillar shares stores, screens, and a unified streak/XP system. That
shared state is the product hook ("your gym AND your meals AND your
friends in one place") but it doubles the surface area for bugs.
Every workout that completes has to update both the workout dashboard
and the social feed and the achievement engine and the XP store. A
bug in one corner cascades across screens.

## App Store: fifteen rejections deep

We got rejected by App Store review **more than fifteen times** before
launch. None of the rejections were about the app being bad — they
were about checklists nobody warns you about: privacy manifest fields,
specific wording in subscription disclosures, screenshot guidelines on
status-bar consistency, IAP receipt validation edge cases.

The lesson: rejections are a process, not an emergency. Each one
teaches you a checklist item that no blog post mentions. Plan for
ten cycles, not one.

## The cost reality

The big cost lines are:

- **OpenAI GPT-4o** — per scan, the dominant variable cost. Mitigated
  by the on-device classifier (which gates the call when confidence
  is high enough), the barcode path (which calls OpenFoodFacts and
  costs zero per scan), and aggressive caching of repeated foods.
- **Supabase** — predictable monthly cost for Auth, Postgres, Storage,
  edge function invocations, and bandwidth. At our scale it is in
  the tens of dollars per month and scales gently with usage.

The unit economics work because Pro and Premium tiers (RevenueCat)
cover the variable costs for users who scan often, and Free users get
gated to the cheaper paths.

## What I&apos;d do differently

**Ship v0.1 to production before chasing v1.0.** The version we
launched in February could have gone out in October with one pillar
(nutrition) and grown from there. I optimized for a polished launch
and lost four months of real-user feedback that no internal review
can substitute. The next product I build will hit the App Store with
a single feature and a clear roadmap.

## For other student builders

If you have been "almost ready to launch" for more than three months:
launch the worse version. Every week you do not ship is a week of
feedback you do not get. The polished v1.0 is rarely worth the
months you spent on it.
