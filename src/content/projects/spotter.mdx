## Overview

Manual food logging is the dominant failure mode of nutrition apps —
friction kills adherence within weeks. Existing computer-vision approaches
guess portions from 2D photos and fail on volume estimation. Since 2020,
every iPhone Pro ships with a LiDAR sensor that nobody had productized for
consumer nutrition.

Spotter is the app I wished existed: point your iPhone at a plate, get
back portion-accurate macros without typing, searching, or scanning
barcodes. The scan flow takes under three seconds.

This case study covers the consumer product. The underlying portion-volume
research is documented separately in my
[undergraduate thesis case study](/projects/tfg).

## Architecture

```
iPhone camera + LiDAR
  └─> ARKit depth + frame capture
       └─> Core ML food classifier (on-device, <100ms)
            └─> Backend aggregates scan metadata
                 └─> GPT-4o multimodal call
                      (image + class hypothesis + LiDAR-derived volume)
                       └─> Structured macros returned
                            └─> Local persistence + history view
```

The key insight: the LLM doesn't have to estimate volume — LiDAR already
did. The LLM only has to reason about food identity and the
macronutrient profile of a known portion. This narrows the failure surface
and produces a much more reliable output than a vision-only pipeline.

## Key decisions

### On-device classification before the LLM call

I considered sending raw frames straight to GPT-4o, but ran the math on
latency and cost. A Core ML classifier identifies likely candidates in
under 100ms locally, and the LLM only has to validate and refine. This
keeps the scan UI snappy and cuts API costs by routing obvious cases
without a full reasoning call.

### LiDAR depth as the volume signal, not photogrammetry

Photogrammetry requires multi-angle capture, which is a UX killer. LiDAR
gives metric depth from a single frame. The portion-estimate quality
ceiling is lower than a Kinect-style 3D scan, but the floor is high
enough for the actual use case (a casual user logging a meal in seconds),
and the UX is uncompromised.

### GPT-4o over a fine-tuned vision model

A fine-tuned vision model is a longer path with worse generalization out
of the gate. GPT-4o handles long-tail foods (mixed dishes, regional foods,
homemade meals) without per-food training data. The tradeoff is per-scan
cost; the mitigation is the on-device classifier filtering trivial cases.
