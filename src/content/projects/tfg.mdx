## Overview

Undergraduate thesis at Universidad Carlos III de Madrid. While
[Spotter AI](/projects/spotter) is the consumer product, the TFG is a
distinct research artifact: an RGB-D nutritional pipeline that uses
the iPhone Pro LiDAR sensor to push portion-volume accuracy closer to
research-grade, with explicit confidence and a reproducible
evaluation harness. The two projects are independent — Spotter uses
pretrained foundation models served through APIs, the TFG trains and
evaluates custom geometry + multimodal architectures end-to-end on
public food datasets.

Defense scheduled for **6 July 2026** at UC3M.

## Research questions

1. How much portion-volume accuracy can a single iPhone LiDAR scan
   deliver compared to lab-grade depth setups (Kinect, structured
   light, photogrammetry)?
2. What is the right division of labor between geometric estimation
   (from depth) and semantic reasoning (from multimodal LLMs)?
3. Can the system produce calibrated per-scan confidence intervals on
   the final macronutrient values, not just point estimates?

## Method

The pipeline takes 36 RGB-D frames captured through ARKit on an
iPhone Pro and produces structured per-segment macros end-to-end:

```
iOS LiDAR (ARKit) → 36 RGB-D frames + camera poses + intrinsics
  └─> TSDF reconstruction (Open3D, 5 mm voxel)         [3D geometry]
       └─> 2D instance segmentation                     [DeepLabV3+ MobileNetV3,
            (per-frame masks)                            FoodSeg103, 104 classes]
            └─> Back-projection to 3D point cloud      [per-instance volumes]
                 └─> Mesh-based volume estimation     [Poisson + divergence
                      (per segment)                    theorem; voxel fallback]
                      └─> GPT-4o classification        [name, category, density,
                           + macros per 100g            macros per 100g]
                           └─> Fusion                  [weight = volume × density,
                                                       macros = weight × per_100g]
```

## Key decisions

### LLM-direct classification over a hand-curated food database

Earlier versions of the pipeline used CLIP ViT-B/32 with LoRA rank=8
fine-tuned on Nutrition5k (~300 scans), plus a hardcoded density table
of ~50 food codes. Both were brittle: CLIP confused visually similar
foods (chicken vs. turkey, white rice vs. couscous), and any food
outside the table received a generic density of 0.75 g/cm³, causing
large compounding errors.

The thesis defends a different architecture: GPT-4o handles
classification, density estimation, and macros-per-100g in a single
structured-output call. This is justified by the literature
(DietAI24 in Nature Communications Medicine, ACETADA 2025), where
LLMs with retrieval over standardized nutrition databases achieve
state-of-the-art accuracy on calorie estimation.

### Mesh-based volume estimation over voxel counting

The original Spotter pipeline counted occupied surface voxels, which
the code itself flagged as "a conservative lower bound — interior
empty voxels are not counted." The thesis replaces this with Poisson
surface reconstruction followed by volume via the divergence theorem,
inspired by VolE (Scientific Reports 2026). Voxel counting is kept as
a fallback for segments with fewer than 50 points.

### iPhone LiDAR over photogrammetry

Multi-angle photogrammetry needs the user to circle the plate, which
is a UX killer for a consumer-grade workflow. LiDAR gives metric depth
from a single scan path. The accuracy ceiling is lower than
structured-light lab setups, but the floor is high enough for the use
case and the UX is uncompromised.

## Datasets and benchmarks

- **Nutrition5k** (Google, 2021) — 5,006 dishes with RGB-D and verified
  weights, used for training and evaluation
- **FoodSeg103** (2021) — 9,490 images across 154 ingredient classes,
  used to train the 2D segmenter
- **MetaFood3D** (CVPR 2024 Workshop) — 743 3D objects with mesh + RGB-D
  + nutrition, used as a 3D volume benchmark
- **FNDDS / BEDCA** — standardized nutrition databases used for ground
  truth on macros per 100 g

## Stack

- iPhone Pro (LiDAR-capable) as the capture device
- Spotter mobile app extended with research-mode flags for raw frame
  capture
- Python backend service: Open3D for 3D reconstruction, PyTorch for
  the segmentation model, OpenAI Python SDK for the GPT-4o calls
- FastAPI for the analysis service
- Docker for reproducible deployment

## Key results

Results are being finalized for the defense in July 2026. The thesis
documents:
- A side-by-side error comparison between voxel-counting and
  mesh-based volume estimation on MetaFood3D
- An ablation of the LLM-direct classification step versus
  CLIP+LoRA+catalog on Nutrition5k
- An end-to-end evaluation of macro mean absolute error on a held-out
  set, with per-class breakdowns and calibration analysis

## What I&apos;d do differently

I would have committed to the final architecture earlier. The first
iterations chained CLIP+LoRA+hardcoded-density-tables together
because each component was incrementally easier; the LLM-direct
architecture is dramatically simpler and required throwing away two
months of work to adopt. Picking the right architecture is worth more
than iterating on the wrong one.
